Mission Overview

TEMPEST is a NASA flagship-class Europa surface mission designed to search for signs of life, assess habitability, and characterize Europa's internal structure. The mission architecture consists of an orbiter and a lander, launching May 3, 2036 on a VEEGA trajectory (Venus-Earth-Earth-Ganymede-Approach) and arriving at Europa on October 13, 2042 — a total flight time of approximately 6.45 years. Upon arrival, a four-Ganymede-flyby pump-down tour reduces Jupiter approach velocity from 5.682 km/s to Europa capture, with a total moon tour ΔV of 3.94 km/s. The lander targets a surface lifetime of one month, with full science data return within the first week post-landing. Total mission budget is capped at $2.8 billion ($3.5 billion with 25% reserves).

My contribution focused on the spacecraft bus structural design for the orbiter and lander, and carrying out all necessary Entry, Descent and Landing calculations. The lander primary structure is a hexagonal aluminum (Al 2219-T87) frame with a wet mass of 1,253.7 kg and a total volume of 7.544 m³, sized to survive launch loads, Europa descent, and surface operations within Jupiter's intense radiation environment — a total ionizing dose of up to 510 krad(Si) over 30 days, mitigated through 15mm aluminum vault shielding. I designed and sized the six Ti-6Al-4V landing legs to withstand a touchdown impact load of 619.2 N per leg at 3 m/s vertical velocity, selecting a 25mm outer diameter with 2.5mm wall thickness for buckling and impact performance, yielding a total leg assembly mass of 2.82 kg. Ongoing work includes modal and thermal analysis of the bus structure in ANSYS to verify frequency separation requirements and confirm thermal margins across the EDL and surface operations phases.
